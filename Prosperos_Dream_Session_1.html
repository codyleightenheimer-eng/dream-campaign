What This Session Does

By the End of Tonight

The crew has a ship, a Debt or credit load, and a reason to be at Prospero's Dream specifically.

They meet Angus — and by the end of the session should have at least a suspicion that he's not as neutral as he first plays.

They're pulled into one of A Pound of Flesh's three storylines — not all three. The other two become rumors and background noise for later.

They walk away with a hook into Death Pays All Debts without it feeling forced.

Session length: one 3–4 hour sitting, built to end on a cliffhanger, not a resolution.

Warden Prep

Before You Sit Down

Print or open the Panic Table and Wounds Table.

Have the UCR Bestiary open in case you need a quick horror on short notice.

Decide which of the three APOF storylines you'll lean into if the table doesn't self-select one (see Step 5).

Know Angus's real allegiance cold, and decide how far into tonight you're willing to let it show. He should read as warm and useful first, unsettling second.

Skim the NPC quick-reference at the bottom of this document — several of tonight's incidental contacts (Loshe, Sem, The Babushka) are drawn straight from it.

Cold Open

Arrival

Don't start with character creation at the table if you can help it — get buy-in on concept beforehand, then open in media res.

Your ship drops out of hyperspace and the Dream fills the whole window — eight million people packed into a structure never designed to hold that many, docking rings lit up like a strand of cheap Christmas lights wrapped around something much older and much darker underneath. Traffic control is backed up. You've got a two-hour wait in the queue before you're even cleared to dock.

That's when the comm crackles. Someone's hailing your ship directly — not traffic control. A voice, calm, unbothered by the wait: "Heard you're new in-system. I can get you queue-jumped in ten minutes. Cost you a favor, not credits. Interested?"

This is Angus, and this is the first thing the crew learns about how the Dream actually works: money isn't the only currency, and someone was watching them arrive before they'd even docked.

First Contact

Angus, the Fence

What changed in this rebuild

Angus is not neutral. He's the sysadmin of canyonheavy.market and, per A Pound of Flesh's own text, "the intelligence arm of the Golyanovo II Bratva" — "loyal to Yandee." He runs the sector's most trusted data brokerage, which is exactly why the Bratva uses him as their eyes and ears.

This doesn't mean he's a villain tonight. It means his warmth has a purpose.

Play him exactly as before — chatty, transactional, evasively cheerful about himself — but every favor he calls in is quietly building an asset for the Bratva, whether or not the crew ever finds that out. He doesn't lie about who he is; he just never volunteers it. If a player directly asks "who do you work for," he can even answer honestly and disarmingly: "Work for? Nobody, strictly speaking. I just know people, and people like knowing I know things." True, and also not the whole truth.

He fixes the queue problem in exchange for the crew owing him "a favor, unspecified, redeemable later." Don't let them pay it off with credits if offered — the favor is the point, and it's your hook into Death Pays All Debts later.

His actual first "ask" should be small and mundane — deliver a package, verify a rumor, watch someone from a distance — and should route the crew toward whichever of the three storylines you've picked (Step 5). He doesn't explain why he wants it done. He never does.

Warden note

Tonight is not the night to reveal the Bratva connection outright. Let it surface later — maybe next session, maybe the session after — as a "wait, that's who he actually works for?" beat. The best version of this reveal recontextualizes something helpful Angus already did, rather than announcing itself.

Picking a Storyline

One Spine, Two Rumors

APOF gives you three interlocking storylines. Run one as the session's spine; the other two exist as ambient noise (overheard conversations, news tickers, graffiti) that can pay off later. Each now has a real named contact to anchor it.

Best if the table leans toward: faction politics, moral ambiguity, "who do we side with"

Option A — The Teamster Strike

The Teamsters Local is agitating against Bratva interests after the Stratemeyer Syndicate captured a fleet of Reidmar's freighters. Angus's favor routes the crew into the middle of it — maybe delivering something to Reidmar directly, maybe "just watching" a confrontation for him. This seeds Reidmar (Local 32819L's Union Rep, and one of the Dream's five power brokers on The Bench alongside Yandee) as a recurring contact, and his daughter Trieu as a minor recurring face at the Hangar Bay. Plants the Teamster/Bratva tension that runs all the way through to Grim Merchants' Local 32819L references later.

Best if the table leans toward: environmental horror, class tension, dread over action

Option B — The Choke Unrest

The Choke — the deoxygenated lower-block zone — is getting worse. Imogene Kane, leader of the Hunglung insurgency, is planning something against Tempest Co.'s guards, unaware that Dr. Bancali's old experiments have left her slowly dying. Angus's favor might be "someone I know went down there and hasn't come back" — a Hunglung contact of his, perhaps Paz Asis, who the crew can find or fail to find. This is your on-ramp to the Choke as a recurring setpiece (it pays off huge in Exit, Pursued by a Dream later) and to the Hunglungs as a real faction, not just a demographic label.

Best if the table leans toward: body horror, ticking clocks, classic Mothership dread

Option C — The ACMD Outbreak

Something is spreading. This is the most self-contained of the three if you want a tighter first session. Don't reveal Dr. Emil Bancali or Caliban by name tonight — just enough that "Caliban is coming" graffiti or a nervous rumor lands and means something later. The full truth (a father, a dying daughter named Ariel, a merger with the station itself) is a gut-punch reveal worth saving for when the crew has more context to feel it.

If you genuinely can't tell which way the table will go: run the cold open and Angus's introduction, then let his first job stay intentionally vague enough that any of the three can absorb it. Improvise the pivot once you see where the players push.

The Session Shape

Structure

Arrival + Angus (30–45 min) — cold open, queue-jump, the favor is asked.

First contact with the storyline (45–60 min) — the crew does Angus's task and it goes sideways, pulling them properly into the chosen storyline.

Escalation (60–90 min) — the storyline's real stakes become clear. Use APOF's actual content directly here — this session shape is scaffolding around it, not a replacement for it.

Cliffhanger close (15–20 min) — end on a decision point, not a resolution.

Seed, Don't Resolve

Threads for Later

  • Yandee — a name that comes up, spoken with real respect or real fear depending on who says it. She is the active Vor of the Bratva, running things from a penthouse the crew has no reason to see yet. Don't let anyone imply she's absent, weak, or hard to reach — quite the opposite. If a player asks around, the honest answer is "you do not want her attention."
  • The Bench — the Dream's five power brokers (Yandee, Reidmar, Cutter, Ukko/Ukka, Chief Adjudicator Brunhildh) effectively run the station's real politics. The crew doesn't need to meet all five tonight — whichever storyline you ran should have introduced one. The other four can just be names on a wall or a rumor for now.
  • The Choke — even if you didn't run Option B, a single unsettling detail should surface.
  • Caliban — even if you didn't run Option C, one piece of graffiti or one nervous rumor should land: "Caliban is coming." Don't explain it.
  • Tempest Co. — a logo on a crate, a mercenary in a bar, something small. Recurring background faction, quietly loyal to nobody but themselves (Cutter is already plotting against Yandee, though the crew has no reason to know that yet).

These threads don't need to matter yet. They need to feel like the world existed before the crew showed up. That's what makes the Dream feel alive rather than staged.

Closing the Session

The Hook Forward

However tonight resolves, the natural next beat routes toward Death Pays All Debts: a heist opportunity involving Harpy Tower, brought to the crew by Angus, framed as either paying off their favor or as a much bigger, much more lucrative ask now that he trusts them. If tonight went well, this should feel like a reward. If it went sideways, it should feel like Angus calling in a debt at the worst possible time — and now the crew has a little more reason to wonder exactly whose debt they're really paying.
