The Pitch

"You've heard the stories. Some of them are even true. Prospero's Dream isn't on any Company map worth trusting, and that's exactly why people come here — to disappear, to make a fortune, to find something nobody's supposed to find. Eight million people packed into a station built for a fraction of that many. Nobody's watching too closely. Nobody's asking too many questions. That's not a warning. That's the opportunity." — recruitment placard, Prospero's Dream arrivals dock

That's the sales pitch. Here's the truth: you're a crew of nobodies trying to make it on a black-market megastation where the biggest players — a Bratva crime syndicate, a corespace rival syndicate, a labor union, a religion, a hacking collective, and a mercenary company — have all been quietly at war with each other for longer than you've been alive. You don't have to pick a side. You will anyway, eventually. That's how the Dream works.

Before You Sign On

Read This Twice

Word from anyone who's lasted a while out here

People die on the Dream. Not because they were careless, usually — the odds here are stacked against you no matter how careful you are. The ones who last are the ones who ask questions before they open doors, and who know when walking away beats winning a fight.

You rarely get to keep everything. Getting out alive, getting to the truth, and getting everyone home safe are three different jobs, and most nights you only get to pick two. That's not bad luck. That's just the Dream.

This station doesn't look away from anything. What happens in the Choke, in the old drydocks, in the places the Company pretends don't exist — none of it gets softened for you. If something in a session isn't sitting right, say so, in character or out. Nobody on a real crew asks you to explain yourself for that.

Who You Can Play

Four ways in. Dozens more once you're through the door.

Every character starts as one of four core types. Where you go from there is up to you and the Warden.

Marine

Handy in a fight — but when you panic, everyone close to you feels it too.

Android

Cold, unnerving, and built different. Other crewmembers don't fully trust you, and that's fair.

Scientist

You know what's wrong before anyone else does. That's not always a comfort.

Teamster

Blue-collar, capable, unglamorous. The person everyone actually needs.

Once you've found your feet, the Dream has room for more than these four — fences, smugglers, ex-convicts, secret agents, corporate lawyers, and worse, each dragging their own flavor of trouble aboard with them. Ask around before you settle on who you're going to be.

Before You Ship Out

What Everyone Wishes They'd Known

"I've never done this before."
Nobody expects you to. Show up with a rough idea of who you are and why you're here — the rest sorts itself out fast once you're actually sitting at the table.
"What should I bring?"
Yourself, and a name if you've picked one. A job, a debt, a reason to be running — anything's enough to start from.
"How bad can it actually get?"
Bad. People who come to the Dream don't always leave it. Keep a second idea in your back pocket, just in case the first one doesn't make it.
"I'm not really a horror person."
This isn't the jump-scare kind. It's the kind that creeps up on you slow, and the table decides together how far to let it go.
"What if something's too much?"
Say so. Doesn't matter when, doesn't matter how. Every crew worth flying with has that rule.
